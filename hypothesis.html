Formative Assessment

Prior to the beginning of my placement, I was asked to predict what I thought I would learn, what I would struggle with, and how it fit into my ideas of career and career progression.

As an administrative assistant for the Burning Man Project, I will be working directly under the office manager for a department of 60 heavy equipment operators, truck drivers, and riggers for roughly a month and a half. Thus, I think the most helpful key skills and capabilities I’ve developed in my time at St Andrews will be my higher-order skills, such as empathy, decision making, and critical analysis. Deming (2022) finds that higher-order skills are increasingly relevant and economically valuable within the modern workplace. Through economics group project leadership, I feel confident in my ability to manage a project and deadlines while still accommodating the needs of others.

In economics, at least within my studies, we spend a large amount of time discussing positive externalities. Even if a decision made by an individual does not seem personally optimal, the positive effect it can have on the workplace and the greater world and community may make it worthwhile. In this sense, I hope to recognize which assignments are being done with the purpose of sustaining difficult to measure metrics, such as camaraderie within employees, or the maintenance of routines. These benefits can help to reduce the wage premium that is often necessary within seasonal or difficult positions. The key debate of how much energy and capital should be allocated into increasing human capital and learning of employees that may not return is highly salient, as my department engages in voluntary trainings for other department and artists. Theoretically, this reduces friction between departments and maximizes returns on rentals, however returns are difficult to measure.

My career goal is to enter the United States foreign service. It’s important to me to feel as if I am doing something worthwhile, and I see the greatest opportunity internationally. I’m very drawn by the possibility of moving to different countries while still being supported by a steady job and housing. I see a lot of similarities between my summer position and the foreign service. Both require the navigation of working and living alongside coworkers and managers, strong levels of resilience and flexibility, and organization amid disordered times. Beyond the social necessities of both jobs, I will be improving my office management skills, and the cold-hard-world of excel tracking. I will be responsible for maintaining an employee notice board, onboarding stray employees from other departments, using effective radio protocol, and taking meeting notes. While not all of these skills will be transferable, I hope that they demonstrate my ability to be confident and adaptable, creative, and an empathetic team-player.

I am excited about my upcoming position. I’ll be working as an administrative assistant for a large organization living in the Nevada desert. I’ve gone out to the desert for a similar time before, but I’m worried about feeling like time is slipping away. Work hours are long and the days are generally unvaried, so it’s easy for it to pass too fast. Whenever I start a new role, I feel like I worry so much about the day to day that I let the weeks pass me by. I want to be more intentional about how I am spending my time and potentially take on a long-term project to keep me focused. At the beginning of my session, I think I should sit down with my supervisor and brainstorm additional tasks I can take on. Hopefully, writing these journal entries will help me reflect and gain hyperopia.

In roles where I am lacking practical knowledge, I tend to stick to the tasks I understand well instead of asking questions and trying to learn. It’s pretty counterproductive, both to me, and to the organization, but it’s hard to swallow my pride, especially when it’s a busy day, and asking for help would mean asking someone else to take time out of their day to show me something. I think I’ll try to make my first journal entry a list of skills I’d like to come out with, so I can hold myself accountable in learning them. In my position it’s too easy to hide away from the heat in my air conditioned office, rather than maximizing my learning potential.
